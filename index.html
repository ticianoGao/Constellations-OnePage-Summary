Student Preparation

College and Career Preparation

  • Student Preparation measures the extent to which the school provides students with access to computer science coursework.
  • College and Career Preparation measures the extent to which the school provides students with access to computer science coursework.
  • For elementary and middle schools, the score is based on the ratio of CS enrolled students to the total enrolled students.

    Readiness score = (CS enrolled students ÷ Total enrolled students)
  • For high and K-12 schools, the score is based on the number of Category 1 (SB108 approved) courses, the number of total students at the school, and 4 years of school education.

    Readiness score = (Approved Category 1 courses ÷ Total students) x 4, capped at 100.

Mathematics Proficiency

  • Mathematics Proficiency measures the total % of students marked as Proficient or Distinguished Learners per the Georgia Milestones End-of-Grade assessments.
  • For elementary schools, the learner percentage is based on the school's percentages of Proficient and Distinguished Learners for Grade 5 Mathematics.
  • For middle schools, the learner percentage is based on the school's percentages of Proficient and Distinguished Learners for Grade 8 Mathematics.
  • For high schools, the learner percentage is based on the school's percentages of Proficient and Distinguished Learners for Algebra Concepts and Connections.
  • Readiness score = Distinguished Learner % + Proficient Learner %

Instructional Capacity

  • Instructional Capacity measures the school's CS teaching capacity relative to the amount of CS course enrollment it serves and its total student population.
  • Instructional Capacity uses three elements:
    (1) the number of CS teachers
    (2) 6 teachable sections from a total of 7 (6 instructional, 1 planning)
    (3) an ideal student-to-teacher ratio of 30.
    The score is the product of these three elements.
    The resulting number represents the total amount of students that a school could accommodate for CS courses in a single school day.

    Readiness score = (# of CS teachers x 6 sections x 30 students per section)
  • Schools with no reported CS enrollment, no reported CS teacher, or unavailable data currently receive a score of 0.

Participation Equity

  • Participation Equity measures how closely the demographic composition of CS enrollment reflects the demographic composition of the school's overall student population.
  • Parity scores are calculated separately for race/ethnicity and gender. A parity score is calculated by comparing each group's share of CS enrollment relative to its share of overall school enrollment, using the percentage of that group's overall enrollment as a statistical weight. Participation Equity is calculated by using the comparing the CS participation rate for each group to its estimated count when applied to the entire student population. The total estimated student count is then compared to the total student population to determine the total weighted participation rate.

    A score of 100 indicates that the CS enrollment distribution closely matches the school's overall student distribution. A lower score indicates a greater disparity between the CS enrollment and the overall student distribution. Larger differences between the two distributions result in lower scores. The Participation Equity score is the average of the available race/ethnicity and gender parity scores.

    Parity score calculation: (Total students) x (Overall school enrollment % of group) x (CS enrollment % of group)

    Readiness score calulation: (Total of parity population scores) / (Total student population)

Course Access

  • Course Access measures the extent to which schools across the selected district provide students with access to computer science coursework.
  • The district Course Access score is the average of the Course Access scores for all schools in the district.

CS Enrollment/Total Enrollment

  • CS Enrollment/Total Enrollment measures the level of CS course enrollment across schools in the district relative to the district's total student enrollment.
  • The district score is the average of the scores for all schools in the district.
  • CS enrollment counts course enrollments rather than unique students, so students enrolled in multiple CS courses may be counted more than once.

Course Progression

  • Course Progression measures the extent to which applicable schools in the district provide students with opportunities to move from introductory CS experiences to broader or more advanced coursework.
  • The district Course Progression score is the average of the available Course Progression scores for High and K-12 schools in the district.
  • If no applicable High or K-12 schools are available, Course Progression is shown as N/A and is excluded from the district's overall CS Readiness Score.

Instructional Capacity

  • Instructional Capacity measures CS teaching capacity across schools in the district relative to the amount of CS course enrollment being served.
  • The district Instructional Capacity score is the average of the Instructional Capacity scores for all schools in the district.
  • Schools with no reported CS enrollment, no reported CS teacher, or unavailable data currently receive a score of 0.

Participation Equity

  • Participation Equity measures how closely CS enrollment participation within schools across the district reflects each school's overall student population.
  • The district Participation Equity score is the average of the Participation Equity scores for all schools in the district.
