  • The CS Readiness Score is a 0-100 measure of how well a school is prepared to provide students with meaningful computer science learning opportunities and resources. It considers factors such as CS course access, student participation, course progression, instructional capacity, and equitable participation. Higher scores indicate stronger and more broadly accessible CS resources for students.
  • The CS Readiness Score is calculated by combining five indicators—CS access, participation, course progression, instructional capacity, and participation equity—with each indicator scored from 0 to 100.
    CS Readiness Score = Access + Participation + Progression + Capacity + Equity 5
  • State average: For school reports, the state average for each component is calculated using all Georgia schools of the same school type as the selected school. For example, when an elementary school is selected, the state average represents the average component score across all elementary schools in Georgia.

Course Access

  • Description: Measures the extent to which the school provides students with access to computer science coursework.
  • How it is calculated: For elementary schools, the score is 100 if at least one CS course is offered and 0 if no CS course is offered.
  • For middle, high, and K-12 schools, the number of Georgia DOE-approved CS courses is compared with the 75th percentile among Georgia schools of the same school type.
  • Score = (School approved CS courses ÷ Peer benchmark) × 100, capped at 100.

CS Enrollment/Total Enrollment

  • Description: Measures the level of CS course enrollment relative to the size of the school's student population.
  • How it is calculated: CS Enrollment/Total Enrollment is calculated as total CS course enrollments divided by total student enrollment.
  • This rate is compared with the 75th percentile among Georgia schools of the same school type.
  • Score = (School enrollment rate ÷ Peer benchmark) × 100, capped at 100.
  • CS enrollment counts course enrollments rather than unique students, so students enrolled in multiple CS courses may be counted more than once.

Course Progression

  • Description: Measures whether students have opportunities to progress from introductory CS experiences to broader or more advanced coursework.
  • High and K-12 schools: The score is based on four milestones worth 25 points each: at least one foundational course, at least one intermediate or programming course, at least one advanced course, and at least three Georgia DOE-approved CS courses.
  • High/K-12 Score = Number of milestones met x 25.
  • Elementary and middle schools: Because detailed course-sequence information is not consistently available, total CS course count is currently used as a proxy for course breadth.
  • The school's course count is compared with the 75th percentile among Georgia schools of the same school type.
  • Score = (School CS course count ÷ Peer benchmark) x 100, capped at 100.

Instructional Capacity

  • Description: Measures the school's CS teaching capacity relative to the amount of CS course enrollment it serves.
  • How it is calculated: The school's CS enrollment-to-teacher ratio is calculated as total CS course enrollments divided by the number of CS teachers.
  • Because a lower enrollment-to-teacher ratio represents greater instructional capacity, the school is compared with the 25th percentile ratio among Georgia schools of the same school type.
  • Score = (Peer benchmark ratio ÷ School ratio) x 100, capped at 100.
  • Schools with no reported CS enrollment, no reported CS teacher, or unavailable data currently receive a score of 0.

Participation Equity

  • Description: Measures how closely the demographic composition of CS enrollment reflects the demographic composition of the school's overall student population.
  • How it is calculated: Separate parity scores are calculated for race/ethnicity and gender by comparing each group's share of CS enrollment with its share of overall school enrollment.
  • A score of 100 indicates that the CS enrollment distribution closely matches the school's overall student distribution. Larger differences between the two distributions result in lower scores.
  • The final Participation Equity score is the average of the available race/ethnicity and gender parity scores.
  • A demographic dimension is not used when an estimated nonzero reporting group contains fewer than 15 students.

  • The CS Readiness Score is a 0-100 measure of how well a district is prepared to provide students with meaningful computer science learning opportunities and resources. It considers factors such as CS course access, student participation, course progression, instructional capacity, and equitable participation. Higher scores indicate stronger and more broadly accessible CS resources for students.
  • The CS Readiness Score is calculated by combining five indicators—CS access, participation, course progression, instructional capacity, and participation equity—with each indicator scored from 0 to 100.
    CS Readiness Score = Access + Participation + Progression + Capacity + Equity 5
  • State average: For district reports, the state average for each component is calculated by averaging the corresponding component scores across all school districts in Georgia. Each district contributes equally to the state average, regardless of the number of schools or students in the district.

Course Access

  • Description: Measures the extent to which schools across the district provide students with access to computer science coursework.
  • How it is calculated: A Course Access score is first calculated for every school in the district using the same methodology as the school report.
  • Elementary schools receive 100 if at least one CS course is offered and 0 otherwise. Middle, high, and K-12 schools compare their number of Georgia DOE-approved CS courses with the 75th percentile among comparable Georgia schools.
  • The district Course Access score is the average of the Course Access scores for all schools in the district.

CS Enrollment/Total Enrollment

  • Description: Measures the level of CS course enrollment across schools in the district relative to school enrollment.
  • How it is calculated: Each school's CS Enrollment/Total Enrollment score is calculated by dividing CS course enrollments by total student enrollment and comparing that rate with the 75th percentile among Georgia schools of the same school type.
  • The district score is the average of these individual school scores.
  • CS enrollment counts course enrollments rather than unique students, so students enrolled in multiple CS courses may be counted more than once.

Course Progression

  • Description: Measures the extent to which schools in the district provide students with opportunities to move from introductory CS experiences to broader or more advanced coursework.
  • How it is calculated: A Course Progression score is first calculated for each school using the methodology appropriate for that school's type.
  • High and K-12 schools receive 25 points for each of four milestones: a foundational course, an intermediate or programming course, an advanced course, and at least three Georgia DOE-approved CS courses.
  • Elementary and middle schools use total CS course count as a current proxy for course breadth and compare that value with the 75th percentile among comparable Georgia schools.
  • The district Course Progression score is the average of the Course Progression scores for all schools in the district.

Instructional Capacity

  • Description: Measures CS teaching capacity across schools in the district relative to the amount of CS course enrollment being served.
  • How it is calculated: Each school's CS enrollment-to-teacher ratio is compared with the 25th-percentile ratio among Georgia schools of the same school type.
  • Because lower enrollment-to-teacher ratios represent greater instructional capacity, lower ratios receive higher scores.
  • The district Instructional Capacity score is the average of the Instructional Capacity scores for all schools in the district.
  • Schools with no reported CS enrollment, no reported CS teacher, or unavailable data currently receive a score of 0.

Participation Equity

  • Description: Measures how closely CS enrollment participation within schools across the district reflects each school's overall student population.
  • How it is calculated: Race/ethnicity and gender parity scores are calculated separately for each school by comparing the demographic distribution of CS enrollment with the school's overall demographic distribution.
  • Each school's available race/ethnicity and gender parity scores are averaged to produce its Participation Equity score.
  • The district Participation Equity score is the average of the Participation Equity scores for all schools in the district.
  • A demographic dimension is not used when an estimated nonzero reporting group contains fewer than 15 students.
